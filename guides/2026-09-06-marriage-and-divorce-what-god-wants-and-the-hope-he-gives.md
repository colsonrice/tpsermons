# Marriage and Divorce: What God Wants and the Hope He Gives

The Urgent Kingdom · Mark 10 · Aaron Brockett

[Message page](https://tpcc.org/messages/marriage-and-divorce-what-god-wants-and-the-hope-he-gives) · [Watch](https://youtu.be/5RB27yO5fIY) · [Listen](https://episodes.captivate.fm/episode/72b097e0-6e8f-46bb-8f1f-19c4665171ea.mp3)

## Recap

The message focused on Jesus' teaching about marriage and divorce in Mark 10. Jesus was confronted by Pharisees who questioned Him about the legitimacy of divorce. He reminded them that while Moses permitted divorce due to hardened hearts, God's original intention was for marriage to be a lifelong covenant. Jesus emphasized that marriage is a divine union, and divorce, though sometimes permissible, should be a last resort. Moreover, marriage is a symbol of the relationship between Christ and the Church, showing the depth of commitment and love God desires between spouses.

## Discuss

### God's Original Design

1. What might be God's purpose for creating marriage as a union between two people?
2. Why do you think Jesus emphasizes marriage as a lifelong covenant?
3. How do your personal views on marriage align with the idea of it as a divine union?

### Addressing Divorce

1. Why did Jesus say Moses permitted divorce?
2. What are the modern challenges that people face in marriage that could lead to divorce?
3. How do we balance the understanding of biblical grounds for divorce with grace and forgiveness?

### Hope and Redemption

1. How does Jesus' response to the woman at the well demonstrate hope in broken situations?
2. Have you experienced or witnessed God redeeming relationships? What did that look like?
3. In what ways can the Church support individuals or couples in pursuit of healing after relational breakdown?

## Take Action

This week, take a specific step to strengthen a key relationship in your life. This could be your marriage, a friendship, or a family connection. Write a note, plan a meaningful activity, or start an open conversation about what you appreciate in that relationship and how you want to cherish it.

## Reflections

- Consider a time when you felt grace and forgiveness in a relationship. What was that like?
- Reflect on your own understanding of marriage as a covenant. How does this perspective challenge or affirm your current views?
- Think about a relationship that might need healing in your life. How can you open your heart to reconciliation?
