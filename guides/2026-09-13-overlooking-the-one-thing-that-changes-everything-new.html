2Childlike Reception

SayThe disciples missed a key point: the kingdom of God belongs to those who receive it like children.

Background

Jesus challenges the disciples to embrace childlike faith, emphasizing a posture of receiving rather than achieving. Children epitomize trust and dependence, traits often lost in adulthood as self-sufficiency builds.

  1. Must ask

    Jesus said the kingdom belongs to the childlike.

    When did you approach faith with the receptiveness of a child?

    Follow upWhat let you drop your guard and openly receive?

  2. Receiving implies letting go of control.

    Where in your life do you insist on achieving rather than receiving?

    Follow upWhat would it cost you to release control in that area?

  3. Childlike faith is marked by trust, not by commandment-keeping.

    When have you defaulted to checklists of faith rather than trust?

    Follow upWhat does it protect you from confronting in yourself?

  4. The story of the children's blessing highlights contrasts with material worries.

    Where does the adult world's pressure rob you of simple trust?

    Follow upWhat fear keeps you clinging to complexity over simplicity?

3Encountering the Rich Young Ruler

SayJesus' conversation with the rich young ruler uncovers what's below his good intentions.

Background

The rich young ruler sought eternal life, confident in his moral achievements. But Jesus knew his heart, pressing him on possessions being a stumbling block to real discipleship. We see our struggle: sincere intentions divided by deeper idols.

  1. Must ask

    The ruler wanted eternal life without letting go.

    What's the one possession or value you won't release for Jesus?

    Follow upWhat deeper fear or identity depends on it?

  2. Jesus loved the young man but asked hard things.

    Who tells you the hard truths out of love today?

    Follow upWhat are you avoiding by ignoring their counsel?

  3. The man's sorrow marked deep attachment to wealth.

    When did a pursuit of security leave you unsatisfied?

    Follow upWhat does that pattern continue to cost you in relationships or faith?

  4. The rich young ruler walked away sad, unable to part with his wealth.

    How have you responded when a pursuit fell short of offering fulfillment?

    Follow upWhat did it expose about your priorities or fears?

4True Discipleship

SayJesus doesn't want your money; He wants your heart surrendered to Him.

Background

True discipleship challenges us to recognize and let go of idols, anything overvalued above God. Jesus, our ultimate example, gave all for us. Identifying idols demands honest reflection on what competes for our hearts.

  1. Must ask

    Jesus challenged rich man's perceived self-sufficiency.

    How do you secure your sense of self-worth outside of God?

    Follow upWhere do you turn when those things fail you?

  2. The disciples sought clarity on salvation beyond wealth.

    What belief about church or faith subtly competes with reliance on Jesus?

    Follow upWhen did it subtly limit your spiritual growth or outreach?

  3. Jesus removed the ruler's escape route to reliance on wealth.

    What's one comfort you hold back from surrendering to Jesus?

    Follow upHow does keeping it preserve an illusion of control?

  4. Jesus made eternal wealth available through His poverty and sacrifice.

    Where can embracing sacrificial generosity reshape your view of security?

    Follow upWhere will retraining trust in God's provision first test your comfort?
