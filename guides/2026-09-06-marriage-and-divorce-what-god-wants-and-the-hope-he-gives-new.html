2Beginning with Brokenness

SayLet’s start by acknowledging that marriage can be messy and redemption stories remind us that God works in brokenness.

Background

The story of Harry and Nellie illustrates how God can bring beauty from the ashes of past mistakes. This sets the stage for understanding the redemptive power of God in our relationships.

  1. Must ask

    Harry's story shows redemption happens beyond initial failures.

    Where have you seen God redeem a situation that seemed hopeless?

    Follow upWhat specific action can you take to invite God into your broken places?

  2. Aaron shared his own family history marked by broken marriages.

    How have your family relationships shaped your views on marriage?

    Follow upWhat beliefs from your upbringing still influence you today?

  3. Divorce often leaves scars, but is not the unforgivable sin, according to Aaron.

    How do you handle lingering guilt from past relationship failures?

    Follow upWhat steps can you take this week toward accepting grace in those areas?

  4. The sermon illustrated that God hates divorce but loves the people involved.

    How has the pain of a past divorce or separation affected you personally?

    Follow upWhat forgiveness have you sought or should you offer?

3Understanding God's Design

SayJesus pointed to God's original intent for marriage, unity and covenant.

Background

In discussing marriage, Jesus emphasized unity and covenant, not just the legality of divorce. God’s design is about lifelong commitment and reflection of His love.

  1. Must ask

    Jesus elevated the conversation to God's original intent for marriage.

    How do you view marriage differently when you consider it a covenant, not a contract?

    Follow upWhat's one change you need to make to reflect this view in your own relationship?

  2. Covenant relationships involve deeper commitment and sacrifice than contracts.

    When did you have to choose commitment over convenience in a relationship?

    Follow upHow does that choice impact your relationship with God or others today?

  3. The true purpose of marriage is often challenged in today's culture.

    Where have you felt cultural pressures against God's design for marriage?

    Follow upHow can you guard your relationship against these influences?

  4. Marriage was described as a reflection of Jesus' relationship with the church.

    How does understanding marriage in this way affect your current or future relationships?

    Follow upIn what situation can you better model this relationship?

4Grace and Redemption

SayGrace allows for second chances. Jesus' interaction with the woman at the well illustrates this redemption.

Background

Aaron reminded us that God's grace is abundant, even after relationship failures, and He offers redemption and hope for everyone.

  1. Must ask

    God offers grace and redemption no matter the past.

    What steps have you taken to embrace grace in your own life?

    Follow upWhat's the next step you need to take in accepting grace?

  2. The woman at the well encountered grace despite her marital history.

    Where are you most in need of Jesus' redeeming grace right now?

    Follow upHow can you actively seek that grace this week?

  3. Jesus calls us to respond to His grace by aligning our lives with God's Word.

    Where might you be resisting aligning your life with God's design for relationships?

    Follow upWhat's one specific change you can commit to making?

  4. Living in God's grace means embodying forgiveness and surrender.

    How do you extend grace and forgiveness to yourself and others regularly?

    Follow upWho in your life needs more grace from you?

  5. Recognition of past mistakes calls for a response of repentance and action.

    When have you experienced the need to turn from a past mistake and make amends?

    Follow upWhat immediate steps can you take to address any unresolved issues?
