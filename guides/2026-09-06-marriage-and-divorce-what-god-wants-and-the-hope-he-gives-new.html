2Marriage: God’s Design

SayLet's begin by looking at Jesus' teaching on marriage as God's original idea and intent.

Background

Jesus responds to the Pharisees' question by emphasizing God’s original design for marriage, citing Genesis. He highlights marriage as a union created by God, beyond human redefinition. This counters the culture's casual view on divorce.

  1. Must ask

    Jesus affirms that marriage is God’s idea, designed for life.

    How have you embraced or resisted God's design for marriage in your life?

    Follow upWhat specific changes could align your life more closely with this view?

  2. Marriage is a picture of Jesus' covenant with the church.

    Where in your relationships can you improve your commitment?

    Follow upWhat needs to change to reflect a covenantal approach with someone in your life?

  3. Anything God creates, Satan counterfeits, aiming to distort marriage.

    When have you felt your marriage or a close relationship under attack?

    Follow upHow did you respond, and what might you do differently next time?

  4. Jesus points to marriage as a celebration of covenantal joy.

    How do you currently celebrate and cultivate joy in your marriage or key relationships?

    Follow upWhat's one thing you could do this week to nurture this joy?

3Dealing with Hard Hearts

SayNext, we’ll look at why Jesus mentions divorce as a concession and how it relates to hardened hearts.

Background

Jesus discusses divorce as a concession due to hard hearts, not God’s ideal. He stresses protection, especially for vulnerable parties. Divorce wasn't the original intent, but it illustrates human failure.

  1. Must ask

    Jesus mentions divorce as a concession due to hard hearts.

    When have you allowed a hard heart to distance you from someone?

    Follow upWhat specific step can you take this week to soften your heart towards them?

  2. Divorce is permitted to protect, not punish; it's not commanded.

    Where have you used protection as an excuse to close yourself off?

    Follow upHow can genuine protection look different from avoidance in your life?

  3. The Pharisees sought to trap Jesus with divisive questions.

    Have you ever found yourself trapped by cultural debates about relationships?

    Follow upHow can you focus on God’s truth rather than societal pressures?

  4. God hates divorce but loves the divorced. He seeks healing and restoration.

    How can past relationship pain inform your view of grace today?

    Follow upWhat's one step towards healing you could take this week?

4Enduring Covenants

SayFinally, let's explore what enduring in covenant looks like, even amidst struggle.

Background

Jesus contrasts marriage as a covenant, not a contract. It's unbreakable and rooted in mutual commitment and love, reflecting God’s enduring relationship with His people.

  1. Must ask

    Marriage is a covenant, not a contract; a mutual commitment based on relationship.

    Have you approached a relationship transactionally instead of covenantally?

    Follow upWhat shift can you make to reflect a covenant approach?

  2. The biblical story of Hosea illustrates unconditional commitment.

    When have you had to show or received unexpected grace?

    Follow upHow did it feel, and how did God reveal Himself through it?

  3. Divorce is the result of sin, yet redemption remains possible.

    What's a relationship segment you believe is beyond repair?

    Follow upWhat's one action of hope you can take regardless of your doubts?

  4. Jesus' covenant with us is not based on contracts but love.

    How does this understanding change your view of failure and forgiveness?

    Follow upWhat can you do this week to express forgiveness in a real way?

If the room...

Defensive reactions to tough questions.

Acknowledge their feelings, redirect to personal experiences rather than ideological debates.

Men sharing past relationship regrets.

Emphasize grace and steps towards redemption, not dwelling on past mistakes.

Quiet participants.

Invite them with personal, low-pressure questions. Encourage sharing at their comfort level.

Someone feels overwhelmed by the topic.

Offer affirmation of God's grace and hope, invite them to listen without pressure to share.

High emotion when discussing divorce.

Allow space for expression, remind the group of God’s compassion and love for the broken.

Steer by these themes
  • Marriage is God's design, not man's.
  • Divorce is a concession to hard hearts.
  • Marriage reflects the covenant between Jesus and the Church.
  • Covenant, not contract, defines lasting relationships.
  • God's grace can redeem broken relationships.
  • Protecting the vulnerable in marriage is key.
  • The importance of humility and forgiveness in relationships.
