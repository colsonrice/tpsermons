Before you begin

The sermon by Aaron Brockett focuses on Mark 10:13-31, where Jesus addresses the rich young ruler. The core tension is between what we hold onto in life versus what we're willing to surrender to follow Jesus fully.

The passage is split into two encounters: Jesus blessing the children and the rich young ruler seeking eternal life. Key themes include the need for childlike receptivity versus adult self-sufficiency, and the challenge of placing possessions above discipleship.

A poignant illustration was the Challenger disaster, where NASA overlooked a critical detail, costing lives. This mirrors the ruler's mistake of valuing wealth over surrender, a decision potentially far more costly eternally.

While the primary topic is wealth, the message is broader: identifying personal idols that prevent wholehearted devotion to Christ. Be mindful of how discussions might touch on sensitive areas like financial security or past financial failures, as highlighted in the sermon.

The sermon repeatedly stresses that salvation cannot be achieved by one's actions but is received through grace alone. Jesus, as a reflection of complete generosity and sacrifice, is the model for us to follow.

Childlike Faith

Jesus contrasts the openness of children with the self-reliance of adults. In Mark 10:13-16, He emphasizes that the kingdom belongs to those who embrace it like children. Childlike faith means relying on God rather than our accomplishments.

1

Where in your life have you found it difficult to trust like a child?

2

Think about a time you had to be vulnerable. What was the hardest part?

3

What does receiving the kingdom as a child look like for you this week?

4

How can embracing humility change your relationship with God today?

If it stalls
  • What specific area of your life needs more childlike faith?
  • How have your perceptions of vulnerability changed over time?

The One Thing

The rich young ruler earnestly seeks what he must do to inherit eternal life, but he misses the point by focusing on his own actions rather than Jesus’ invitation to surrender. Jesus identifies his wealth as the barrier to wholehearted commitment.

5

What's something you value that competes with your devotion to Christ?

6

Reflect on an area where you've chosen security over surrender. What happened?

7

When has achieving replaced receiving in your spiritual life?

8

How might letting go of a 'functional savior' bring freedom?

If it stalls
  • What is the actual cost of holding onto this 'one thing'?
  • Who could support you in surrendering this area?

True Riches

Jesus redefines wealth in terms of eternal treasure. When Peter points out the disciples' sacrifices, Jesus promises returns beyond earthly value. The challenge is to fix our eyes on heavenly reward instead of earthly wealth.

9

Where have you settled for immediate comfort over future gain?

10

Think about a time you prioritized eternal impact over immediate gain. What did it require?

11

How can you identify where your current treasure is stored?

12

What specific steps can you take this week to invest in eternal value?

If it stalls
  • What earthly 'riches' are hardest for you to view as temporary?
  • Who in your life exemplifies investing in eternity?

Get honest

What will realistically stop you from surrendering your 'one thing' this week?

Closing and application

Name one 'functional savior' you will surrender or one act of generosity you will commit to this week.
