Before You Begin

Leader notes. Read these ahead of time. They are for you, not to be read aloud.

The sermon explores how easy it is to overlook essential aspects of our faith journey, drawing a parallel with the NASA Challenger disaster, where a small oversight led to substantial consequences. Similarly, the rich young ruler in Mark 10 overlooks the one thing that truly matters: complete surrender to Jesus.

Key sections of the sermon include Jesus blessing children, the conversation with the rich young ruler, and Jesus’ teaching on wealth and entry into the kingdom of God. Each section challenges different attachments we may have, be it wealth, status, or personal achievements, that prevent us from receiving the kingdom of God like a child.

Pastoral sensitivities include financial stress and personal idols. The rich young ruler's story is a real-life application about how possessions can become obstacles to spiritual growth and salvation. The sermon highlights that salvation is impossible with humans but possible with God, challenging us to examine our own lives for areas of resistance and pride.

Opening (10 minutes)

Read Mark 10:13-31, focusing on Jesus' interactions with the children and the rich young ruler.

Then pick one icebreaker based on your group.

Option A (a newer group)

Think about a time you had to give up something significant. How did it feel at the moment, and what was the result?

Option B (a group of storytellers)

Share a story of a time when you misunderstood someone's intentions. How did you find clarity?

Option C (a high-trust group)

Reflect on something you once considered indispensable but later found unnecessary. How did that realization change you?

Section 1: Receiving Like a Child

Jesus emphasizes receiving the kingdom with the openness and humility of a child. This section challenges us to let go of the need to earn God’s favor through actions alone.

Have you ever struggled to accept help because you wanted to do it all yourself?

Think of a recent time when your self-reliance was challenged. What did you learn about yourself?

When have you needed to let go of control to fully receive something valuable?

Section 2: The One Thing

The story of the rich young ruler highlights how clinging to material possessions can keep us from fully following Jesus. Jesus asks us to release what holds our heart more than God.

Where have you settled for less because holding on seemed safer?

What one thing today, if Jesus asked for it, would be hard for you to release?

How have your possessions or desires shaped your identity more than your faith?

When have you found joy in letting something go that was once precious to you?

Section 3: Possible Through God

Jesus reassures his disciples that while entering the kingdom seems impossible by their standards, with God, all things are possible. This section encourages us to trust in God’s ability over our own.

Recall a time when you attempted the impossible. What role did faith play?

What area in your life needs a fresh reminder that 'with God, all things are possible'?

How can you rely on God's strength in an area you feel weak this week?

Section 4: True Surrender

True discipleship requires surrender, which is not just about giving away material things but fully submitting to God’s will. This section examines what surrender means practically.

Where do you find it hardest to submit to God’s lead in your life?

What small step might you take this week to practice surrender?

How has past surrender led to unexpected blessings in your life?

Closing and Application (5 minutes)

Each man names one area of his life where he will practice surrender this week and how he plans to do it.

Prayer: Invite the group to pray for courage to identify and release anything holding them back from fully following Jesus.

These are for you to steer by if the discussion drifts. Do not read them aloud.

  • Surrender and trust in God are essential parts of discipleship.
  • Material possessions can become obstacles to following Jesus fully.
  • Faith is about receiving, not achieving.
  • True security and identity come from God, not material wealth.
