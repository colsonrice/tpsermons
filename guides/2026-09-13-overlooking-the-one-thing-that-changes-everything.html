Before You Begin

Leader notes. Read these ahead of time. They are for you, not to be read aloud.

Aaron opened with a powerful illustration of the Challenger disaster, pointing out how a small oversight led to catastrophic failure. This story framed the sermon by illustrating how something seemingly minor, when overlooked, can result in massive consequences. The rich young ruler had everything outwardly but missed the critical internal change, just as NASA ignored the critical warning about the O-ring.

The passage in Mark 10 captures a moment when Jesus exposes the limitations of human achievement in favor of divine grace. Jesus asked the rich young man to let go of his wealth, not as a rejection but as a love-driven invitation to true discipleship. Aaron's focus was on the contrasting ideas of doing to earn versus receiving through grace.

Tonight's guiding thesis is that the 'one thing' we often hold is what holds us back. Follow the sermon’s three-part outline: the confrontation of reliance on possessions (verses 13-22), the difficulty of kingdom entry for the self-reliant (verses 23-27), and the promised reward of surrender (verses 28-31). Each section encourages a fresh confrontation with what we might avoid releasing.

For this group of high-functioning men, polished answers can mask deeper resistance to change. Lean into the personal, pressing them to identify the 'one thing' they are clinging to. Tonight earns its keep in the section on surrender, where vulnerability around letting go of control or status might emerge.

First, someone might feel shame over financial struggles or past decisions. Approach this with sensitivity, providing one-on-one space later if needed. Second, if someone’s one thing isn't material wealth but personal status or emotional security, allow room for that to surface. Third, be ready for emotional responses when discussing past sacrifices or failures; the go-around should help process discomfort constructively.

Opening (10 minutes)

Assign two readers. First reads verses 13-22, focusing slowly on the rich young ruler's question and Jesus' response. Second reads verses 23-31, emphasizing Jesus' message about wealth and salvation.

Then pick one icebreaker based on your group.

Option A (lower trust group)

What's one item you'd struggle to lend out to someone and why?

This question eases into the evening's theme by discussing personal attachments to possessions.

Option B (storytelling group)

Describe a time when holding onto something ultimately held you back.

Encourages sharing personal narratives of struggles with letting go.

Option C (high trust group)

What's a comfort you prioritize that you're ashamed to admit?

Prompting vulnerability about personal comforts can mirror deeper spiritual dependencies.

Section 1: The Rich Young Ruler (12-15 minutes)

In Mark 10:13-22, a young man approaches Jesus with a sincere question about inheriting eternal life. This man, though adhering to the commandments, misses the one thing Jesus knows holds his heart, his possessions. Jesus invites him to sell all and follow, exposing a tough truth about reliance and true discipleship.

The rich young ruler believed his achievements and adherence to the law made him 'enough' in God's eyes.

When have you relied on accomplishments or rule-following for identity rather than your relationship with God?

Jesus looked at the man and loved him, despite knowing his heart's conflict.

Who are you reluctant to confront truthfully due to comfort or fear of conflict?

The young man walked away sad, choosing wealth over discipleship.

What have you chosen over a deeper commitment to Christ?

The rich man's question was sincere yet revealed a misunderstanding of inheritance.

When have you tried to achieve something that should have been received freely?

Jesus asked the man to give up everything to follow Him.

What is the most difficult thing you need to let go of to follow Jesus more fully?

Section 2: Jesus' Hard Teachings (10-12 minutes)

In verses 23-27, Jesus speaks to the disciples about the difficulty for the wealthy to enter God's kingdom, using the metaphor of a camel and the eye of a needle. This shocks the disciples, prompting a revelation about possibilities with God versus human achievement.

Jesus emphasizes that human effort cannot earn entrance into God's kingdom.

Where are you attempting to earn God's favor rather than relying on His grace?

The disciples were amazed at the standard Jesus set and questioned who could be saved.

What teachings of Jesus have you found most challenging to accept or implement in your life?

The impossibility of salvation through human means is only made possible with God.

How have you witnessed God making the impossible possible in your life?

Jesus uses wealth to illustrate deeper heart issues.

What does your attitude towards money reveal about your heart's priorities?

Section 3: Surrender and Reward (10-12 minutes)

In verses 28-31, Peter reminds Jesus that the disciples have given up everything to follow Him. Jesus assures them of the sure rewards for sacrifices made in His name, reinforcing that the first will be last and the last first, highlighting the upside-down nature of God's kingdom.

Peter highlights the sacrifices made by the disciples.

What sacrifices have you made for your faith, and what have been the personal costs and rewards?

Jesus speaks of receiving a hundredfold for those who have left everything for His sake.

Can you recall a time when giving up something for God led to unexpected blessings?

Jesus' promise of rewards comes with the inclusion of persecution.

How do you navigate the tensions between sacrifice and the persecutions that may follow?

Jesus' teaching flips human notions of greatness and reward.

Where might you be clinging to status or recognition, and how can you shift those priorities?

Closing and Application (5 minutes)

Each man names the 'one thing' revealed to him tonight that he needs to surrender and states a specific, actionable step to achieve this before we meet again.

Prayer: Each man prays a simple sentence asking for strength to surrender his 'one thing,' and the leader closes by reading through the promises found in Matthew 6:19-21 about treasures in heaven.

Key Themes to Reinforce

These are for you to steer by if the discussion drifts. Do not read them aloud.

  • Possessions and Discipleship: The tension between worldly wealth and following Jesus wholeheartedly.
  • Grace over Achievement: Salvation is received, not achieved, underscoring the importance of grace.
  • Personal Surrender: Identifying and surrendering the 'one thing' that might hinder spiritual growth.
  • Divine Possibility: Recognizing that with God, what seems impossible to us is made possible.
  • Reward and Sacrifice: Jesus' assurance of eternal rewards to those who sacrifice for His kingdom.
