# New edition

Built to be **led live from a phone**, not studied. A leader glancing down
mid-conversation should find his next move in two seconds. Same sermon, same
standard of questions as the classic guide, organised around running the room.
No timings anywhere.

**guide_title**: a short thematic title of two to five words naming the
sermon's central tension.

**goal**: one sentence naming what tonight should accomplish.

**thesis**: the single sentence from the message the leader keeps in his back
pocket all night. Use the preacher's own central claim where it is clear. It
must make an argument about God or about us, as a full sentence. Never just
restate the sermon's title.

**outline**: two to five short lines stating the preacher's own outline or
moves, in order, as action phrases ("Serve without an agenda"). These are the
preacher's moves, not the names of your sections, so they must not repeat the
section titles.

**sensitivities**: zero to three short, specific flags about what may be raw
for someone tonight and how to handle it. Each under twenty-five words.

**read_refs**: one or two short verse ranges with verse numbers, such as
"Mark 10:13-16". Never a bare chapter and never the whole sermon passage. The
key verses the message turned on: four to ten verses each, twelve at most. If
the message covered a long passage, pick its two most important short ranges.

**scripture_instructions**: one or two sentences on who reads which range and
anything to let land before talking.

**icebreakers**: exactly three, labelled A, B, C, each with "fits" (newer
group, storytellers, high-trust group), the question, and a short "note".

**sections**: three or four, following the sermon's structure. Each has:
- "title": a short name.
- "say": one or two sentences the leader can read aloud word for word to move
  the room into this section, in a natural spoken voice.
- "setup": three or four sentences of background for the leader, kept tight.
- "questions": each with a lead, an ask, a "probe" (one short follow-up that
  pushes a surface answer toward the specific week, the named person, the real
  cost), and "star": true on exactly one question per section, the one to keep
  if time runs short.

**obstacle**: one "Get honest" question asked near the end: what will
realistically stop this man from following through this week. Friction, not
theory.

**closing_go_around**: a concrete go-around script the leader can read aloud.

**prayer**: brief, specific prayer instructions.

**carry**: one sentence naming what the group asks each other next week.

**cheat_sheet**: five to seven rows of "dynamic" and a short "response" the
leader can act on instantly.

**key_themes**: five to seven short lines to steer by.

**commitment_prompt**: closes the participant sheet: one specific step this
week and the man who will ask about it.
