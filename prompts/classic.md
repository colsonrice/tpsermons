# Classic edition

The group's established leader guide. Mirror its structure exactly. It reads
like a document the leader studies beforehand, roughly three to four printed
pages, and is also used live.

**guide_title**: a short thematic title of two to five words that names the
sermon's central tension, distinct from the sermon's own title.

**goal**: one sentence naming what the night should accomplish, ending in a
specific personal outcome for this week.

**leader_notes**: five substantial paragraphs, roughly 500 to 700 words in
total and never fewer than 400, for the leader alone. Each paragraph is five
to eight full sentences with concrete detail from the sermon. Build them in this order:
1. The sermon's opening illustration or story, told with enough detail that a
   leader who missed Sunday can retell it, and why it frames the passage.
2. The turn in the passage: what actually happens in the text and the
   preacher's read of what was underneath it, anchored with one or two of his
   phrases.
3. The thesis, the one sentence the leader should keep in his back pocket all
   night, followed by the preacher's outline stated plainly. Say that the
   outline is the spine of the guide.
4. A word on this specific room: how this passage is aimed at capable,
   self-managing men, where polished answers will show up, and which section
   is where the guide earns its keep.
5. The pastoral sensitivities, numbered in prose (first, second, third): what
   could be raw for someone tonight, anything kept out of the discussion
   questions on purpose and why, and how to handle it privately. Pastoral
   sensitivities live here and nowhere else.

**scripture_instructions**: how to read the passage aloud. Split a long
passage between two readers by verse range, and note any verses to read
slowly and let land before anyone talks.

**icebreakers**: exactly three, labelled A, B, C. Each has "fits" (A suits a
newer group with lower trust, B suits storytellers, C suits a high-trust
group), the question, and a one-sentence "note" to the leader on why it works
or how to keep it. Every icebreaker connects to the sermon's theme.

**sections**: three or four, following the sermon's structure. Each has:
- "title": a short name for the movement.
- "minutes": an estimate such as "12-15"; the sections together fill 35 to 45
  minutes, with the last one tighter.
- "setup": three to five sentences grounding the section in the verses, told
  through the preacher's material, naming the verse range.
- "questions": each with a lead and an ask, as described in the shared rules.

**closing_go_around**: a named, concrete go-around tied to the preacher's
outline: each man names the point that hit him hardest and one specific step
before the group meets again, concrete enough to be asked about in seven days.

**prayer**: specific instructions, for example each man praying one sentence
for the man who spoke before him, and the leader closing with a supplemental
passage the preacher used.

**cheat_sheet**: five to seven rows of "dynamic" and "response": silence after
a hard question, tangents, one voice dominating, surface or Sunday-school
answers, an emotional moment, and a theological rabbit trail specific to this
sermon. Responses are short, tactical, and written for this group.

**key_themes**: five to seven lines, each "Short label: one-sentence
explanation", for the leader to steer by if discussion drifts.

**commitment_prompt**: closes the participant sheet: one specific step this
week and the name of the man who will hold him to it.
